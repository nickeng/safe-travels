# Packing / What to Bring

When a destination has non-obvious packing needs — weather extremes, altitude, cultural dress
expectations, gear for specific activities, or things that are hard to buy locally — include a short
"what to pack" element. Skip it for destinations where packing is obvious (a temperate city break
needs no list). Every item should earn its place by being non-obvious or tied to a specific reason.

Design the element to fit the guide's aesthetic — match the destination's visual identity.

## Two patterns

**Checklist** — the default. A titled list of items, ideally as real checkboxes the reader can tick.
Each item may carry a short italic reason ("Headlamp — pre-dawn trailhead", "Waterproof jacket —
daily mist"). For longer lists, group under small category labels (Essentials / Gear / Documents).
Use this when the items apply across the whole trip.

**Contextual** — group items by activity or situation rather than category ("Dawn photography:
tripod, headlamp, gloves" · "Below-rim hiking: 2–3L water, salty snacks, trekking poles"). Use this
when the trip has distinct activity modes that each need different gear. It doubles as a reminder of
what the days involve.

## "Don't pack" items

Telling travelers what to leave behind is as useful as what to bring (e.g. "skip the umbrella —
locals don't, it's mist not rain"). Show these as struck-through / de-emphasized items within the
list (a checked-and-disabled checkbox with strikethrough text reads clearly as "deliberately not
needed"), or as a short "Skip" group at the end. Keep the reason — the *why* is the whole point.

## Title

Always give the element a heading ("What to pack", "Don't forget") — an untitled list of items
floating in the intro reads as unfinished.
