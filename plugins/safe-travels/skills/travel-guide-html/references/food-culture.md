# Food culture

Start from a base that works for any food-rich city, then let the structure grow with the depth of
the cuisine.

## The base

- **What to eat** — a grid or illustrated list of the local dishes. Each dish carries:
    - its **local name** (include native script  where non-Latin)
    - one vivid line on what it is and why to seek it out (<100 chars)
    - an **availability** tag when it's limited ("winter only", "Thursdays")
- **When locals eat** — cover the full arc of the day's eating occasions, breakfast through the
  late-night snack, so a traveler can plan around local hours; include the secondary occasions
  (afternoon tea, a late-night supper), not only the main meals. Lead with a point of view (which
  meal is the main event) and capture the character, not just the clock; keep each occasion to a line
  or two. Each occasion carries:
    - its **localized meal name** in the everyday register (include native script where non-Latin)
    - its **time window**
    - **what's eaten**
    - optional: the **venue type** it's eaten at (e.g. "Bistro", "dai pai dong") — omit it if the destination's venue types are self-explanatory

## The tables

Group the places to eat around the strongest narrative — the angle that highlights what's special about
eating here (soul food, the day's catch off the boats, a late-night ritual). A long
set rides in a **rail** (`class="cards rail"`) — keep it under ~12 items. Use a **catch-all rail**
("the rest of the table") for what no group claims, rather than forcing unrelated places under a flat
label ("coffee & fine dining").

When one of these groups is a distinctive tradition worth reading about in its own right — not just a
label — promote it to a **thread** (below): a narrative lead over its own dishes and tables.

## Threads — where the culture runs deep

Some places have a food tradition worth diving into — a ritual like the Georgian supra, an institution
like the Hong Kong cha chaan teng, or a repertoire like Oaxaca's moles. A single dish or craft can
anchor a thread too — the gelato worth crossing town for — especially when the traveler asked for it.
Where a tradition or dish is genuinely distinctive, give it its own fun-to-read thread.

A thread bundles everything about that tradition in one section — don't scatter it:
- **its story** — a short editorial lead on the tradition, from the food researcher's Food Culture
  notes; lift its most distinctive point into a callout/sidebar when it earns the emphasis;
- **its dishes** — the signature dishes that belong to it, pulled out of the base grid;
- **its tables** — its restaurants, as cards; a long set rides in a rail (see **The tables**).

A thread's dishes and tables move into the thread, so the base grid holds only what no thread claims.
