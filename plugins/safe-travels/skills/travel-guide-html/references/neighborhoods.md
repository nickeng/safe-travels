# Neighborhoods

In large cities or spread-out regions the base can shape the whole stay. Help the reader pick the right
base for them, then let the stays follow.

## Help the reader choose

Give each option a glanceable card in a compare strip — who it's best for, its vibe, price band, what's
nearby, and any other detail that will help the reader decide. When the bases trade off on several
dimensions, or there are more than three choices, use a comparison `<table>` instead — cleaner than cards
when the rows share the same few columns.

Beneath the choices come the stays in a rail; note on each card which neighborhood it's in. Use two
rails when there's a genuine decision for the reader — aligned with the neighborhood choice (inside vs.
outside town) or cutting across it (what kind of stay). Aim to keep rails between ~4-10 items each.

## Threads — when the neighborhood is the experience

Sometimes the neighborhoods aren't just bases to choose between — each is a distinct experience worth
reading in full. Then escalate from the compare strip into **neighborhood threads**: each gets its own
short narrative lead, a practical callout, and its own rail.

Thread as a **balanced set of 2–3 equivalent neighborhoods** — each drives a distinct narrative, is
genuinely different from the others, and holds ~3+ stays. Not one (a single narrative rail beside a
pooled remainder reads lopsided — keep everything pooled instead), and never more than three (past that
the choices blur — fall back to the compare strip).

Each thread's callout:

- **On your doorstep** — what's in walking distance;
- **Getting around** — the nearest station/line and rough times to the other areas;
- **Trade-offs** — the honest catch (price, noise, distance, polish).
